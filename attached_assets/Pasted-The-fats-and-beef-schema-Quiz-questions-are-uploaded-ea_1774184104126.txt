The fats and beef schema

Quiz questions are uploaded each independently one at a time. The students(also referred to as users) must fetch these questions according to what they need for their academic growth. This is the work of the fats and beef schema. The words fats and beef don't have a very great derivation. The following questions are fetched. 
*Own class - 7 questions (refer this to as usual)
*Lower class - 2 questions (refer this to as revision)
*Upper class - 1 question (refer this to as stretch)
         This keeps learning forward and revision tackled whereas research is not left behind. 
          On top of that, the fats and beef schema is not random at picking questions from subjects as enrolled by user. This avoids unnecessary content while providing wholesome academic questions based on the student's enrolled subjects necessary for the student's academic growth. 
            Imagine a big pool of quiz questions where students get their quizzes from. Each student must ably gat what they need as mentioned earlier. The fats and beef schema does it all. But before we go that deep, it'd be right to first explain the curriculum of the school for which I'm building this project. 
*There is lower school division. This is senior one to senior two(mostly going to refer senior to as S and the class as a figure)
*There is middle school which is S3 and S4. 
*Upper school, S5 and S6. 
                 Lower school division studies 12 subjects where the one subject of the 12 is chosen by them. 
                 Middle school 9 subjects where 2 of those they chose themselves. 
                 Upper school 5 subjects where one of the five is dependant on the combination they chose. Combinations consist of four subjects. Take an example of upper school doing subjects a, b, c, d, and e. The combination goes abc/d where d is dependant on the subjects a, b, and c. Subject e is compulsory also called general paper. Hope it's well explained. 

Cycles

Already explained, each school division does a specific number of subjects. For the fats and beef schema to be effective, it must have cycles of quizzes. A cycle is a completion of receiving questions from each subject in each context, i.e. usual, revision and stretch. 

Middle school cycle

Take an example with middle school which has 9 subjects, the goal is to make all 9 subjects from stretch, usual, and revision. 
*Usual - 7 questions=7×9=63
*Revision - 2 questions=2×9=18
*Stretch - 1 question=1×9=9
Therefore, a middle school cycle(going to be referred to as mcycle or middle school cycle) =90 questions. In a nutshell, a user having made 9 quizzes must have covered all their enrolled subjects in usual, revision and stretch. 

Lower school cycle
*Usual - 7 questions=7×12=84
*Revision =2×12=24
*Stretch =1×12=12 which altogether sums up to 120 questions. 

Upper school cycle
               Upper school has subjects a, b, c, d, and e as demonstrated earlier but in quizzes, subject e, the compulsory is left out. This is because there's no way it needs short answers. 
*Usual =7×4=28
*Revision =2×4=8
*Stretch =1×4=4 which altogether gives us 40 questions. 

To sum up, lcycle(lower school cycle) requires 120 questions, mcycle 90 questions and ucycle(upper school cycle) needs 40 questions. Like I threw light earlier on how quizzes are not just random, there is going to be a reference for the different quiz formation.

Contingency 

There is a fall back mechanism because the fats and beef schema is too large and most likely to get stuck somewhere. The contingency is made for users with the issue of lack of enough quiz questions to make even a single complete quiz of a set of one questions. This may be because user has out done the work of the teachers by doing more quizzes than what the teachers even upload since only one quiz questions can be uploaded at a time yet 10 are done at once(outgrowth). 

Before explaining the contingency, we need to first understand deeper about how the quizzes work. Once a question is drawn from the pool and the quiz is answered and submitted, that question is flagged as done. Flagged off questions will not (not never) appear again unless there is a failure. 

The fall back mechanism(preferably contingency cycle or ccycle) states it that when one is at least ¾ way their cycle and reach the time of outgrowth, the questions in that cycle will all be flagged as undone and thrown back into the pool. This creates ample time for teachers to upload more quiz questions whereas the student is doing their own work still. 
         However, if the current cycle the student is doing is less than ¾ way, then, the questions in that cycle and the ones in the previous one will all be flagged as undone in order to create more time for teacher's upload. Remember that even when the unflagged(previously flagged) questions are thrown back into the pool, the selection of questions to appear in the new quiz is till random. It's not like the thrown back questions will still be the only ones to appear. They may or may not appear. 

When the contingency cycle repeats itself 10 times, then, the last two quiz cycles will be flagged undone.
